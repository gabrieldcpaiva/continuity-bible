# Skool launch kit — The Continuity Bible v1.0

Copy you can paste, plus the sequencing that makes it work. Everything here is written to be **true as of
today**: v1.0, 43 pages, no users yet, US$29 test price, no testimonials. Do not add proof you do not have.

Placeholders in `[brackets]`. Keep your own voice; cut anything that sounds like a funnel.

---

## 1. The strategy in one page

**Do not open with a sales post.** Skool communities reward the member who helps, not the member who
launches. The sequence that fits how your people already trust you:

| # | When | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Day 0 | **Value post**: the 15-item preflight checklist, free, no pitch, no link. | It is genuinely useful on its own, it demonstrates the method instead of describing it, and it gets saved. |
| 2 | Day 0–3 | **Two DMs** to people who comment on it, asking for 30 minutes with the blank sheets. | This *is* your pending two-person usability test. You were going to need two filmmakers; they just volunteered. |
| 3 | Day 3–5 | **Launch post**: what it is, what it isn't, price, and "ask me for the sample". | By now the room has seen the checklist work. The post is a follow-up, not a cold pitch. |
| 4 | Day 5–9 | **Three DMs** to people who asked for the sample, asking the three commercial questions. | This *is* your pending three-person commercial test. Three real answers replace a spreadsheet of guesses. |
| 5 | Day 9+ | One **follow-up post** only if you have something real: a fixed typo, a new page, a quote with permission. | No "still available!" posts. Silence is better than nagging. |
| 6 | Ongoing | Pin the launch post; put the checklist in your Classroom/Resources if you have one. | Members find things months later. A pinned post keeps earning. |

Two honest notes about using your own community as the test bed:

- **Your people are biased toward yes.** They like you, so their enthusiasm is not demand. Count only
  concrete things: money offered, or a *named* sequence with a date. Everything else is "maybe".
- **Try to get at least one of the three from outside the community** (someone in your DMs from another
  platform, a collaborator, a client). Then the tally is 2 insiders + 1 outsider and you can say so in the
  write-up. Do not merge these answers with the usability sessions — separate sheets, separate people
  where possible, recorded separately.

## 2. Post 1 — the value post (no pitch)

> **Title:** The 15 checks I run before I press generate (steal this)
>
> I lost a whole night last month to a mug.
>
> Shot 4 had the mug upright, handle left. Shot 5 came back with the mug on its side, coffee ring on the
> cue sheet, and the lamp in a different corner. Nothing in the prompt had changed. The model had just…
> decided.
>
> The fix wasn't a better prompt. It was a list I could read in sixty seconds, in the same order, every
> time. Here it is, stripped of context — use it on whatever you're generating this week:
>
> 1. **Row check** — the shot number and slug match the row I'm working from. (Wrong row is the most common cause of a wrong shot.)
> 2. **Identity** — the five face anchors are pasted word for word, same order. Paraphrasing anchors is how faces drift.
> 3. **Never-change list** — my three character items and three location items are in the prompt.
> 4. **Variant code** — the wardrobe/light code in force matches the previous shot, or the change has a code and a cause. An uncoded change is drift you haven't noticed yet.
> 5. **Wardrobe** — same garment set, same fastening state, same sleeve length. Sleeves and buttons flip first.
> 6. **Hair** — same part, same tuck, same length. A changed tuck reads as a different person at wide framing.
> 7. **Hands and props** — correct hand, correct prop state code, other hand accounted for. Models love to swap hands.
> 8. **Prop state** — mug, paper, tools, carried items in the state the row records. A prop that resets is a break the audience feels.
> 9. **Geography** — two landmarks named with their frame side; nobody mirrored.
> 10. **The line** — the camera is on the same side of the line as the previous shot. If not, it needs a written reason.
> 11. **Light state** — the light code matches the register, *including what is off*. An unlit practical is continuity too.
> 12. **Time band** — nothing in frame records an impossible time. No clocks, no screen times, no lock screens.
> 13. **Framing** — size, height, angle, movement match the row. Height drift is subtle and expensive.
> 14. **Handoff** — I say the handoff line out loud: "this shot must hand off ___ to shot ___." Saying it catches what skimming misses.
> 15. **Open items** — any unresolved mark is decided, or its accepted risk is written down. Allowed to stay open; not allowed to stay silent.
>
> Two things I learned using it: **check 14 catches the most**, and **check 11 catches the ones you don't
> notice until the edit**. Everything else is maintenance.
>
> Steal the list, no credit needed. If you want the one-page version I print, say so in the comments.

**Why this post works:** it gives away the most immediately usable piece. No link, no price, no "DM me
for the course". The last line invites a comment, which is what feeds step 2.

## 3. Post 2 — the launch post

> **Title:** I built the continuity sheet I kept wishing existed (six shots, one page each)
>
> Three weeks ago I posted the 15 checks I run before generating a shot. A few of you asked for the
> printable version, so I built the whole system behind it. It's called **The Continuity Bible** and it's
> out today at **US$29**.
>
> **The problem it solves.** Six generated shots can look like six different films, and it's almost never
> the model. It's that nobody wrote down what has to survive the cut. So the whole thing is three layers:
>
> - **Identity** — what must never change: five face anchors, five fixed landmarks, a time band.
> - **Variant** — every deliberate change, with a code, a cause and an end (V2 cardigan open, P-01b mug tipped, L2 lamp off).
> - **Shot state** — what's true in *this* shot: action, screen position, hands, prop states, framing,
>   and one handoff line saying what the next shot must inherit.
>
> Every shot row starts by copying the previous row's handoff line. That single habit is the product.
>
> **What's in the folder**
> - 43-page print-ready PDF (A4) — read and annotate
> - The same seven documents as editable Markdown — print, edit, rename
> - Character sheet, location sheet with a floor plan, six-shot state table (three panels), 15-item preflight, drift log
> - A complete worked example: one invented scene, six connected shots, one intentional change traced from the shot that starts it to the shot that ends it
> - A spreadsheet tracker (CSV) and an offline HTML form if you'd rather type than write
>
> **What it is not.** Not a prompt pack, not a model integration, no footage, no images, and no promise
> that generated shots will match. Continuity is a production judgment; this is the written record it
> works from, plus a way to catch a problem before the shot is paid for. It's written to be model-agnostic.
>
> **The honest part.** This is v1.0 and it is three weeks old. **Nobody outside my own desk has used it
> yet.** I'm selling it at a test price of US$29 because I want to know whether it's worth that, and I'd
> rather find out from you than from a spreadsheet. If it doesn't earn its price in your first sequence,
> [30-day refund, no questions — *only write this if you're willing to honour it*].
>
> **How to decide:** don't buy it off this post. Comment **sample** or DM me and I'll send you the worked
> example plus two blank sheets. Read them, then decide. If it's not for you, tell me why — that's worth
> more to me than the sale.

**Two rules:** put the price in the body, not the title or a pinned comment (it reads like a pitch), and
never say "limited spots" unless you actually limit them.

## 4. Post 3 — tester recruitment (run this between posts 1 and 2)

> **Title:** Looking for 2 people to break this (30 minutes, free copy)
>
> I'm finishing a continuity worksheet for six-shot sequences and I need two people who *haven't* seen it
> to use it cold, while I watch and take notes. Not a demo — a test. I want to find out where it's
> confusing before I charge anyone for it.
>
> **What you'd do (about 50 minutes, on a call or by screen share):** I give you a one-line scene brief and
> the blank sheets. You plan six shots with them. I stay quiet unless you're completely stuck, and if I do
> help, I say so out loud so it lands in my notes.
>
> **What you get:** the finished PDF free, and first refusal on anything I make next. **What I get:** the
> truth, including "I'd keep this in my head instead" if that's what you think.
>
> Two slots, ideally people who've generated a sequence of four or more shots in any tool. Comment or DM.

This is your **usability test, run honestly** — and it produces the notes `validation/usability-test-2-filmmakers.md`
asks for. Fill that record in the same day, in their words, not yours.

## 5. DMs

Skool DMs are short. One message, one question, no links before they ask. If they don't reply, that's an
answer — leave it.

**5.1 — To someone who commented on the checklist post**
> Hey [name] — thanks for the comment on the preflight list, [specific detail they said]. I turned it into a
> full six-shot system and I'm looking for two people to use it cold before I put a price on it. Would you
> be up for 40 minutes this week? You'd plan six shots with the blank sheets while I take notes. Free copy
> either way, and I'd rather hear "this is confusing" now than after someone pays.

**5.2 — To an active member you haven't spoken to (permission first)**
> [Name] — quick one, and no is fine. I built a continuity worksheet for six-shot sequences (character
> sheet, location sheet, state table, preflight). Would it be alright if I sent you the sample and asked
> two questions about it? Ten minutes, by reply is fine. I'm trying to find out whether it's worth selling,
> so "not for me" is a useful answer.

**5.3 — To someone who has posted about continuity/drift problems**
> That drift post from [month] is exactly the problem I've been chewing on — the [specific detail]. I built
> a written handoff system for it. Want the sample? No charge and no pitch: read it, tell me where it would
> fall apart in your workflow.

**5.4 — Follow-up after 3 days (only once)**
> No pressure at all — just closing the loop so I don't leave you hanging. If you'd rather not, say the
> word and I'll drop it. [one line about the specific thing you'd want their take on]

**5.5 — When someone says "this looks great" (turn opinion into a fact, gently)**
> Glad it reads well. Can I ask the blunt version: would you actually run it on a sequence this month —
> which one? I'm keeping count of real uses, not compliments, so "probably not right now" is fine and
> genuinely useful.

**5.6 — When someone says no**
> That's useful — thank you. One question and then I'll leave it: was it the format (PDF/sheets) or the
> problem it's solving that missed? I'm rewriting the next version and I'd rather aim it at something real.

## 7. Assets to post (screenshots from your own PDF — no design work)

1. **The seam page** — "The change, end to end" (V2 / P-01b / L2 traced through three panels). Best single image you have.
2. **The filled floor plan** — six camera circles, all on one side of the line. People screenshot this.
3. **One filled Panel B row pair** — shot 4 "hands off", shot 5 "inherits", side by side. Shows the habit in one glance.
4. **The weak vs strong handoff line** — the mono block. Quotable on its own.
5. **The blank preflight** — pairs with Post 1.

Post them as a carousel in Post 2, or one per day in the week after. Cropping tight beats full pages.

Keep one table; fill it the same day, never from memory. This is the commercial test in
`validation/commercial-stopgo-test.md` — three people, three questions, one decision rule.

| # | Name | In community? | Saw the sample? | Anything new to them? | Named a sequence + date? | Buy at $29? | Next step they took |

| 1 | | | | | | | |

| 3 | | | | | | | |

**Decision rule, fixed now:** advance if **1 of 3 would buy at $29** *or* **2 of 3 named a sequence they'd
use it on this month**. "Probably", "looks great", "keep me posted" = maybe, not yes. If nobody hits it,
say so in the community and change the product — a public "nobody wanted it yet, here's what I'm changing"
post buys more trust than a silent retreat.

## 9. Do not say

- "Tested by filmmakers", "professionals are using it", "5 stars", "as featured in".
- "Guarantees consistency", "eliminates drift", "never drifts", "perfect continuity".
- "Limited to 10 copies" unless 10 is real.
- "Works with [model]" as a compatibility claim.
- Anything about Milanote or Boords prices except the verified figures in `research/research-note-2026-11-23.md`
  (and they may have changed since 23 Nov 2026 — re-check before quoting).
- "DM me for the link" as the only call to action — put the price and the ask in the post; DMs are for the
  people who want the sample first.

## 10. Three things I'd do that you didn't ask for

1. **Add the refund line before you post.** One sentence, honoured without arguing. It removes the only
   real objection a $29 PDF has, and it is the cheapest credibility you will ever buy. It's currently
   missing from the product, and that was deliberate — it's your call to make, not mine to invent.
2. **Say the price out loud, once, in the post.** Hiding it until the DM makes the whole thing feel like a
   funnel, and your community will smell it.
3. **Ask your two testers for one sentence you can quote, with their permission and their name.** That
   sentence is worth more than any copy in this file — and after your first real quote, the next launch
   post writes itself.
