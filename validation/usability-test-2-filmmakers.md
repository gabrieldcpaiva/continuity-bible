# Two-person usability test — workflow usability

**Status: prepared, not run.** No filmmaker has been observed using this product. Nothing in this file is a
result. When it has been run, fill in the record at the bottom with what actually happened, including the
parts that went badly.

**What it tests:** can two filmmakers plan a new six-shot sequence with these sheets, unaided, without
reading the whole book first?

**What it does not test:** whether anyone will pay for it. That is the separate three-person commercial
test in `commercial-stopgo-test.md`. Do not count a person from this test as a participant in that one
unless they complete both activities and both are recorded separately.

---

## 1. Who to recruit

- **Two filmmakers** who have generated at least one multi-shot sequence themselves — any tool, any level.
- Recruit them **independent of each other**: two separate sessions, no shared screen, no discussion
  between them before the second session.
- They should **not** have seen the product, this file, or the worked example before the session.
- Explicitly ask permission to take notes and to quote them anonymously. If they decline notes, do not run
  the session.

**Sourcing.** Community members who volunteer after reading a post are the easiest honest source. If they
already know you, note it — it affects how much help they will ask for. Copy for recruiting them:
`launch/skool-launch-kit.md` §4.

## 2. What to send before the session (10 minutes of their time)

Send only these three things, as files:

1. `02-character-sheet.md` (blank)
2. `03-location-sheet.md` (blank)
3. `04-six-shot-state-table.md` (blank)

Do **not** send the worked example, the field dictionary, or the usage guide. The test is partly about
whether the sheets stand alone.

Include this message:

> Thanks for doing this. I am testing a continuity worksheet for short generated sequences, not testing you.
> Before we meet, please look at the three files for ten minutes and note anything that is unclear. Please do
> not read any other material I have sent you in the past. In the session I will give you a one-line scene
> brief and ask you to plan six shots with these sheets. I will not help unless you are completely stuck, and
> if I do help, I will say so out loud so we can record it.

## 3. The scene brief to give at the start of the session

Read this aloud, verbatim, and let them keep a copy:

> **Scene brief.** One character, one room, six shots, no dialogue. A night-shift security guard in a
> small car park office watches a figure walk past the window twice. The second time, the figure stops.
> You have no budget and no crew: everything will be generated.

Deliberately chosen because it has a prop with a state (a torch, a mug, a radio), a light state (office
light on or off), and a natural change (the guard stands up). Do not suggest any of that.

## 4. Session script (about 50 minutes, one participant at a time)

| # | Task | Time | What you do | What you record |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Warm-up | 5 min | Ask what they usually do to keep shots consistent. Do not mention the product's method. | Their current method, in their words |
| 1 | Six beats | 10 min | Ask them to write six beats for the brief, in the table or anywhere. | Time taken · whether they wrote beats or prompts |
| 2 | Cast and place | 12 min | Ask them to fill the character sheet and the location sheet "enough to use". | Which fields they skipped and why · where they paused |
| 3 | Six-shot table | 15 min | Ask them to fill the table for shots 1–6, including the handoff lines. | Whether shot 1's handoff line appears in shot 2's "inherits" field · how they handled a change |
| 4 | Preflight | 5 min | Hand them the preflight checklist and ask them to run it for shot 3. | How long it took · which checks they read out loud · which they ignored |
| 5 | Debrief | 8 min | Ask the four debrief questions below. | Verbatim quotes |

**Rules of conduct:** think-aloud encouraged; no teaching; if they ask "what does this mean", answer only
"what do you think it means?" and log the question. If they are fully stuck for more than three minutes,
help them, and log "helped" with the exact words you used.

## 5. Debrief questions (ask in this order, verbatim)

1. In your own words, what is the difference between the character sheet and the table?
2. Where did you have to guess what a field wanted?
3. Point at the moment you would have stopped using this on a real project.
4. If this were on your desk next week, what would you remove?

## 6. Pass / revise rules (decide before the session, not after)

Treat the test as a **pass** only if all four are true:

- Both participants complete tasks 1–4 without help.
- Both can explain identity vs variant vs shot state, in their own words, without reading the field dictionary.
- Both write a handoff line into the next shot's "inherits" field without being told.
- Neither is blocked for more than three minutes on any single field.

Treat it as **revise** — and revise before any commercial test — if any of these happen:

- Both stall on the same field (that field is the problem, not the person).
- Either cannot name the difference between the four status markers after using them.
- Either produces a table with no handoff lines, or with handoff lines that contradict the next row.
- Either says some version of "I would keep this in my head instead".
