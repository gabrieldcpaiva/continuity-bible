# Three-person commercial stop/go test

**Status: unvalidated. Not run.** No participant has been contacted, shown the product, or asked about
price. The US$29 figure is a **test price**, not a finding, and the decision rule below is a **proposal**,
not a result.

This is a **separate activity** from the two-person usability test. Different question, different
participants, different record. A person who did both counts once in each activity only if both are
written up separately — never as two people in one activity.

---

## 1. The question this answers, and the one it does not

- **Answers:** among three independent AI filmmakers, is there a plausible signal to keep going — either
  one person willing to buy at US$29, or two who would put it on a real sequence next week?
- **Does not answer:** willingness across a market, price elasticity, whether the product is any good,
  or whether it can be sold at scale. Three people cannot do that.

## 2. Decision rule, fixed in advance

Advance (keep developing, and try a real presale) if **either**:

- at least **1 of 3** says yes to buying it at **US$29** with a concrete next step (asks how to pay, asks
  when it ships, or offers a card), **or**
- at least **2 of 3** say they would use it on a real sequence in the next month and can name that sequence.

Stop or rework if neither happens. Do not move the goalposts after the calls: a polite "this looks great"
is not a yes, and "I'd probably use something like this" is not "I would use it on a real sequence" unless
they name the sequence.

## 3. Who to recruit

Three people, **independent of each other and of the usability testers**. Criteria:

- has finished at least one AI-generated sequence of four or more shots, or is mid-production on one;
- is the person who would spend the money (no team approval needed);
- has not been shown this product or its drafts before.

If you cannot find three such people, the honest record is "recruitment blocked", not "test passed".

## 4. The exact materials to show

Send one link or one folder containing **only** these four items:

1. `product/The-Continuity-Bible-v1.0.pdf` — the full book, or `06-worked-example-the-third-caller.md`
   plus the two sheets if the PDF is inconvenient. The point is: **one complete sample**.
2. `product/spreadsheets/six-shot-tracker-blank.csv` — **blank sheet one**.
3. `product/02-character-sheet.md` — **blank sheet two** (or its PDF pages).
4. Nothing else. No pitch deck, no testimonials, no roadmap.

Do not attach this file. Do not explain the method before they read.

## 5. Outreach message (copy this, keep it short)

> Subject: ten minutes on a continuity worksheet (no pitch)
>
> Hi — I have made a short written system for keeping six generated shots consistent: one character sheet,
> one location sheet, a six-shot state table, and a preflight list. It is a document, not an app.
>
> Could I send you the sample and the two blank sheets and ask three questions? Ten minutes, by reply or
> on a call, whichever you prefer. I am trying to find out whether it is worth selling, so I would rather
> hear "no" than a compliment.

If they say yes, send the four items and the three questions below in the same message, so they can answer
in writing without a call.

## 6. The three questions (ask in this order, do not reword)

1. Looking at the sample, is there anything in it you could not have written yourself? *(Tests whether the
   product has content, not just formatting.)*
2. Would you use this on a real sequence in the next month? If yes — **which one**, and what would you
   open first?
3. It is going to be **US$29** as a one-off document. Would you buy it at that price — and if yes, what
   would you expect to happen next?

Then stop talking. Do not justify the price, do not offer a discount, do not say "it's also good for…".

## 8. How to record the answers honestly

- "Maybe" is not a yes. If someone says "probably", write **maybe/probably** and do not count it toward
  either threshold. Note the exact words.
- If someone asks for a feature before buying, record the request and count them as **no** for this test.
- If someone would use it but only if a colleague fills it in, that is not a yes from the buyer; record it
  as a **no** with the reason.
- Keep the raw replies (screenshots or emails) with the scorecard. The quotes are the evidence; the tally
  is the decision.
- No payment is taken during this test. If a participant offers to pay, say the product is not on sale yet
  and record that offer as the strongest available signal, separately from the tally.

## 9. What would make this test invalid

- fewer than three people, or three people who know each other and compared notes;
- showing the product with an explanation of how to use it (that measures the explanation, not the product);
- counting compliments, "looks useful", or a request to be notified as a purchase intent;
- changing the price during the test, or offering it free "just to see if they use it";
- running it before the two-person usability test has been passed, so the sheets are still known to be
  confusing.

## 10. After the test

- **Advance:** price test is still a hypothesis. The next step is a real presale attempt — a page that takes
  money — not another round of opinion interviews.
- **Rework:** change what the "no" answers point at, then re-run with three **new** people. Do not re-ask
  the same three after a change; they have already been shown the old version.
- **Stop:** record it here and say so plainly in the product README. A stop is a result.

## 11. Sourcing from a community you already serve

If the three participants come from a community where you are known, say so in the write-up, and treat the
result as **soft evidence**, not market proof:

- **The bias is toward yes.** People who like you will say something kind. Only count a **clear yes**
  (offers to pay, asks how, names a date) or a **named sequence**.
- **Aim for two insiders and one outsider.** Sourcing all three from your own community is allowed, but
  write "3 of 3 from my community" in the record and discount accordingly.
- **Ask behaviour, not opinion.** "Which sequence, and when?" beats "does this look useful?".
- **Do not merge these three with the usability testers.** Different question, different record; if a
  person did both, they count once in each activity only because both were documented separately.
