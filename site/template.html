The mug must
stay there.

Six generated shots, one scene. The face, the label, the mug, the light, the side of the room. Six chances for a model to quietly change one of them and cost you a night.

This is the piece of paper you write before you generate — three layers, one handoff line between every pair of shots, and a check you can run in sixty seconds. The paper does not stop the drift. It names what the next shot must keep. It's a document, not an app. It works with whatever you generate in.

01

You do not have a model problem. You have a nobody-wrote-it-down problem.

You can prompt beautifully and still get six different films. Shot 1 has the label facing camera; shot 4 has it backwards. Shot 2 has the mug on the left; shot 5 has it on the right, full again. Nothing drifted because the model is bad — it drifted because the only place that information lived was your head, and your head was busy.

So write it down once, before you generate, in three layers. Then every shot starts by copying the layer above it instead of describing it again from memory.

Layer 1 · identity

What must never change

Five face anchors, five fixed landmarks, the time band. Written once, pasted word for word, never paraphrased.

brow scar, right · mole, left cheek · bob tucked right ear only
door frame-right · corkboard top-left
Layer 2 · variant

What changed on purpose

Every deliberate change gets a code, a cause and an end. An uncoded change is drift you have not noticed yet.

V2 cardigan open, from shot 5
P-01b mug tipped, ring on the blotter
L2 lamp off, corridor lit
Layer 3 · shot state

What is true in this shot

Action, body position, hands, prop states, framing, and the line that hands all of it to the next shot.

centre-right, 3/4 frame-left · MCU
pencil in the left hand, never the right
hands off to shot 5: same frame, mug full

Every field carries one of four letters: F fixed, C changed on purpose (name the code), U unresolved (say what you'll accept), N/A not applicable (say why). Letters, not colours, so it survives a greyscale print and a colour-blind check.

04

The change, end to end

The worked example in the book changes three things at one seam — thirty seconds of screen time between shot 4 and shot 5 — and traces each change from the shot that starts it to the shot that ends it. That trace is the whole trick: a change is only safe to generate when you can point at every place it is recorded.

V2

Cardigan open, sleeves pushed to the elbows. A silhouette change, readable at medium and wide.

P-01b

Mug on its side, 20 cm ring on the blotter, never on the paper. A state, not an animation.

L2

ON AIR lamp dark, corridor strip lit under the door. A switch described as what is off.

The example is invented and the book says so on the colophon. It survives 86 mechanical checks for contradictions — order, codes, frame sides, the 1↔6 bookend, the mug run — because an example that contradicts itself teaches the wrong habit.

What this is not

Not a prompt pack, not a model integration, not a promise that your shots will match. Continuity is a production judgement; this is the written record it works from, plus a way to catch the problem before the shot is wasted. No footage, no stock images, no third-party templates.

Free for everyone. Print the files, edit them, and use them on the next scene. If a field is wrong, say so.
