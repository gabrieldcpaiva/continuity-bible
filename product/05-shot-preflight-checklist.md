Read this with your hand on the button. It takes about a minute, and it is the only part of the system you use on every single shot.

## Row and identity

- [ ] **Row check** — Wrong row is the most common cause of a wrong shot.
- [ ] **Identity** — Paraphrasing anchors is how faces drift.
- [ ] **Never-change list** — Your personal tripwires.

## Wardrobe, hair, hands and props

- [ ] **Variant code** — An uncoded change is drift you have not noticed yet.
- [ ] **Wardrobe** — Sleeves and buttons are the first thing to flip.
- [ ] **Hair** — Hair tuck changes read as a different person at wide framing.
- [ ] **Hands and props** — Models love to swap hands.
- [ ] **Prop state** — A prop that resets is a continuity break the audience feels.

## Geography, light and time

- [ ] **Geography** — Mirroring is invisible until the cut.
- [ ] **The line** — If it is not, the shot needs a written reason.
- [ ] **Light state** — An unlit practical is continuity too.
- [ ] **Time band** — No clocks, no screens with times, no phone lock screens.

## Framing, handoff and open items

- [ ] **Framing** — Height drift is subtle and expensive.
- [ ] **Handoff** — Saying it out loud catches what skimming misses.
- [ ] **Open items** — Allowed to stay open. Not allowed to stay silent.

Each check names the failure it prevents, because a check whose purpose you cannot remember is a check you will skip. Tick the box, or leave it blank and write why — a blank box is information too.

# The line to read out loud

Before generating, say this sentence with the shot numbers filled in. It is the single habit that catches the most mistakes, because it forces you to name what the next shot is relying on.

```
“This shot inherits  ______________________________________________
 from shot ____ , changes ________________________________________ ,
 and must hand off ______________________________________________
 to shot ____ .”
```

> **Rule — If you keep it, the table changes**  
> A shot you keep is now the truth. If it differs from the row, edit the row — and if it differs from the previous shot, either re-generate it or add the change to the next row's ledger with a code. Never leave the document describing a shot you did not make.

## Printing this checklist

Print six copies for a six-shot scene and write the shot number at the top of each. If you are working entirely on screen, copy the checklist into your notes app once and duplicate it per shot. The boxes work either way: [ ] becomes [x].
